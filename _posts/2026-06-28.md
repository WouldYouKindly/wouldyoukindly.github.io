I went to my 2nd session of [Passionate Politics](https://passionatepolitics.neocities.org/), where we read an excerpt from ["Glitch Feminism" by Legacy Russel](https://legacyrussell.com/GLITCHFEMINISM). I liked it a lot, and had a couple of thoughts.

## Not wanting to understand a different person

There was a woman in the meeting, a queer, disabled, fat, polyamorous woman. She arrived a bit late, and she was wearing a mask.
When I first saw her, I was somewhat annoyed: I didn't like that she was breathing loudly because of the mask, and also the
mask was making it a bit difficult to understand her (and I already have trouble understanding English speakers sometimes.)
But also, because she was a queer, disabled, polyamorous, and fat woman, I had a preconception of her as someone agressive
(it's a pattern I noticed among people with these characteristics) and very different from me.

At one point she said something, and I asked her a question, and it turned out that I really asked the question in order to
answer it myself ("What's your favourite color? Oh, and mine's blue, <and blah-blah-blah>".) It occurred to me that even
though there were many points at which I could answer a genuine question - she's a very different person from me - I really
didn't want to. I didn't want to understand her. I wanted her to dissapear - not because I didn't like her as a person, but
because she was almost the opposite of me: a white cis man. Her being there and talking meant that being a fat queer woman
was just as OK as being a white cis man. We were equal. And by giving power (or legitimacy) to her, it took power away from
me.

It's probably because she was being quite assertive and self-confident that she scared me in this way. Before her, I was
a good WCM trying to expand my bubble and look at different people. After she joined, I became unimportant, just another
dude no one was interested in.

So it was a combination of her taking up the group's attention, and of her being very different from me that scared me.
I didn't understand her, but other people did, and they had discussions in which I couldn't participate. Whereas when
another dude was talking, I could relate to his experiences better, and I could participate so I didn't perceive him
as threatening.

## Residue of Patriarchy

One girl mentioned a male colleague that said something like "She laughs like a man". The girl read it as him thinking
that women are inferior to men. I suggested that it's not a binary: I may think that men and women are equal, but I come
from a society that doesn't believe that, and so in some ways I still view men as superior. We called it the residue of
patriarchy, and it can be extrapolated to other stuff: gayness, transness etc. It's a reminder that people can be on a path,
and we shouldn't label someone as homophobic etc if they happen to make a remark that "betrays" their "real" views. We
contain multitudes, ha-ha.
