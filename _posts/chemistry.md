## Understanding relative atomic mass

Why would I need an average atomic mass? If a take a particular atom, it's mass it never going to
be the average - it's either an isotope A or isotope B, and it has the mass of that isotope.

But chemists don't operate with single atoms. They use large quantities of atoms, and the distributions
of isotopes in these large quantities are the same as in the samples that were used to calculate
relative atomic mass. So if you have a big batch of atoms, and you know the RAM, you can calculate
the number of atoms in the batch by weighing it. That's why you need RAM.
